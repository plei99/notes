                                      1. Introduction
1.1. Historical overview. Mathematical interest in Gromov-Witten theory was
sparked by the predictions of [COGP92], which predicted the number of genus 0
curves of all degrees (precisely, the genus 0 Gromov-Witten potential F0 ) in the quin-
tic threefold using mirror symmetry, which is a duality between two superconformal
field theories known as the A-model and the B-model.
    Later, Gromov-Witten theory, which computes A-model invariants, was developed
in symplectic topology by Ruan-Tian [RT95] and in algebraic geometry by Behrend-
Fantechi and Li-Tian [BF97; LT98] and the mirror formula for F0 of semi-positive
complete intersections in projective toric varieties was proved by Givental and
Lian-Liu-Yau [Giv96; Giv98a; LLY97; LLY99]. A key insight was to relate the
Gromov-Witten theory of the complete intersection Z ⊆ XΣ defined by the vanishing
of a section of a vector bundle E with the so-called twisted GW theory of the pair
(XΣ , E), which is known as the Quantum Lefschetz principle.
    In higher genus, Bershadsky-Cecotti-Ooguri-Vafa [BCOV94] studied the B-model
and deduced a Feynman rule, which computes Fg from the lower genus Fh<g and
a finite ambiguity. Later, Yamaguchi-Yau [YY04] studied the consequences of the
BCOV conjecture in the A-model and deduced that
    (1) All of the Gromov-Witten potentials Fg for Z5 ⊂ P4 , Z6 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 1, 2),
        Z8 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 1, 4), and Z10 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 2, 5) lie in a ring R of polynomials
        in five power series in the Kähler parameter;
    (2) The Fg satisfy a set of partial differential equations called the Yamaguchi-
        Yau equations.1
Huang-Klemm-Quackenbush [HKQ09] used these results and new techniques to
compute the Gromov-Witten invariants of the quintic for g ≤ 51. Unfortunately,
the mathematical formulation of BCOV theory is very difficult and has only been
completed for the elliptic curve [CL12b; Li11].
   Unfortunately, the Quantum Lefschetz principle fails in higher genus g > 0.
Based on the idea of Li-Vakil-Zinger [VZ08; LZ09] to desingularize the “main
component” of the moduli space of stable maps to P4 , Zinger [Zin09] proved the
BCOV conjecture for the quintic in genus 1. This result was later reproved by
Kim-Lho [KL18] using quasimap wall-crossing [CK20], which is another way to
restore the Quantum Lefschetz principle in genus 1.
   A better way to restore the Quantum Lefschetz principle is to introduce the
so-called p-fields, following Chang-Li [CL12a]. They consider stable maps to P4 , but
with an additional section
                                  p ∈ H 0 (C, O(−5) ⊗ ωClog ).
The upshot is that the virtual cycle of Chang-Li’s moduli space coincides with the
virtual cycle of the moduli space of stable maps to the quintic up to an explicit sign.
Using a compactification of this moduli space, Guo-Janda-Ruan [GJR17; GJR18]
proved first the BCOV conjecture for the quintic in genus 2 and then in all genus,
pending the proof of a virtual localization formula in log geometry.

   1Often, mathematicians call these equations the Holomorphic Anomaly Equations. This is
technically inaccurate because in physics, the B-model partition function takes the form FgB (t, t)
and has an antiholomorphic part. The physical Holomorphic Anomaly Equations govern the
antiholomorphic part of FgB (t, t).

   The p-field construction realizes the Gromov-Witten theory of the quintic (and
other toric complete intersections) as a mathematical version of a gauged linear
sigma model (GLSM). GLSMs were introduced by Witten [Wit92] in physics and
by Fan-Jarvis-Ruan [FJR18] in mathematics. As a theory based on geometric
invariant theory, a GLSM may have several phases corresponding to different GIT
stability chambers. Physically, these correspond to different points on the so-called
Kähler moduli space, which controls the A-model. The two phases of the GLSM for
the quintic threefold are known as the geometric phase and the Landau-Ginzburg
phase, which is a theory of the singularity ([C5 /µ5 ], x51 + · · · + x55 ). The enumerative
theory for the LG phase was developed by Fan-Jarvis-Ruan [FJR13], Chang-Kiem-
Li [CKL21], and Polishchuk-Vaintrob [PV16].
   A geometric construction for varying the Kähler parameters and connecting
different phases of a GLSM is the theory of Mixed Spin P -fields, developed by
Chang-Li-Li-Liu [CLLL19; CLLL22] based on the master space construction of
Thaddeus [Tha96]. There are two key facts about MSP theory:
    (1) It has a virtual localization formula which decomposes the theory into three
        parts: the geometric phase, Hodge integrals, and the LG phase;
    (2) The total MSP theory behaves like the Gromov-Witten theory of a Fano
        variety, at least at genus 0.
Using the second fact, a key insight of Guo was to duplicate the field which corre-
sponds to deformation of the Kähler parameter. This idea was realized in [CGLL21]
as N -Mixed Spin P -field theory2 and was used by Chang-Guo-Li [CGL21; CGL19]
to prove the Yamaguchi-Yau conjectures and the BCOV conjecture for the quintic
threefold.
   In this paper, we will study the structure of the Gromov-Witten potentials
Fg for all g ≥ 0 for the targets Z6 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 1, 2), Z8 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 1, 4), and
Z10 ⊂ P(1, 1, 1, 2, 5) using MSP theory. Below, we will outline the results and
methods used to prove them.
